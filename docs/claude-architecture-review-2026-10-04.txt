CLAUDE INITIAL REVIEW

# Architecture review

Labels: **[F]** fact from your brief, **[H]** my hypothesis, **[R]** recommendation, **[M]** measurement needed. All numeric gates are proposals to calibrate against your baseline, not benchmarks.

## 1. Phone efficiency and reliability

**Profile first.** Moving ASR to the Mac is an unverified fix [F], and nothing shows what dominates heat now.

- **Ablation [M]:** run a scripted 2-hour session in four modes: BLE drain only, plus persistence, plus upload and polling, plus UI visible. Log battery %/h and `thermalState`, and use Instruments (Time Profiler, Energy Log) and Flutter DevTools rebuild counts.
- **Suspects [H]:**
  - Whole-library serialize and encrypt on every save is O(library) per write and worsens daily.
  - `ChangeNotifier` rebuilds driven by BLE-rate updates.
  - Opus decode plus base64 archive writes.
  - Radio wake from 2s polling and WAV upload (about 1.9 MB per section, 115 MB per audio-hour).

**Top three changes [R]:**

1. **Replace the whole-library JSON with an incremental encrypted store.** Use SQLCipher or per-record files plus an index, with row-level writes. This also removes the risk that one bad write corrupts everything. Throttle BLE-to-UI notifications to about 1 Hz with granular listenables. Even if profiling shows it isn't the heat source, it is a scaling and durability defect.

2. **Make Opus the canonical artifact and decode on the Mac.**
   - Store raw packets in a length-prefixed binary container with sequence number, device time and a per-page checksum. Drop the base64 JSON (33% overhead) and the WAV.
   - Decode on the phone only for playback.
   - Dual-write Opus and WAV for one to two weeks, and switch only when Mac-decoded audio gives equivalent WER on your gold set. Don't require bit-exact PCM, since decoders can differ.
   - Treat the 60s sections as transport units only. Fixed cuts split words, so I expect ASR errors to cluster at section edges [H, M]. The Mac should stitch contiguous sections into a timeline and let VAD choose ASR windows. Conversation boundaries then become timestamps, which also solves the "cannot split inside a section" limitation.
   - Re-key idempotency to the packet-sequence range plus ASR options.

3. **Design for iOS suspension instead of fighting it.**
   - Use `bluetooth-central` background mode with state restoration for the drain, and a background `URLSession` for file uploads. Verify that works with your LAN, ATS and local-network-permission setup [M].
   - Stop polling: use SSE or long-poll in the foreground and fetch nothing when backgrounded.
   - Treat pendant flash as the first durability tier. Measure its capacity in hours [M], since that bounds how long the phone can safely be suspended.
   - Set a retention policy. The phone is currently the only audio archive [F], and the WAV it stores is unbounded. Make the Mac the Opus archive, with backup, and have the phone delete after a hash-verified receipt plus N days.
   - Use WireGuard or Tailscale to fix both plain HTTP and the phone not always reaching the Mac. Run the API under launchd.

A phone ASR fallback stays deferred. Durable queueing is the right offline answer.

## 2. Mac pipeline and scheduling

**Staged pipeline [R]:**

- **Per-section (minutes):** ingest, stitch, VAD, large-v3. Nothing else runs on this path.
- **Per-conversation (async job):** Qwen3:8b for boundaries, topics and candidate claims. Make topics a queued job with automatic retry and backoff; the synchronous call that strands `topics_pending` is a design bug [F].
- **Nightly batch:** a larger open model (30–70B class, quantized) for consolidation, contradiction checks and the daily review. Choose it by evaluating Vietnamese extraction quality on your own data [M]. I won't quote benchmarks.
- **On demand:** the same large model for chat.

**Scheduler:** with 128GB, memory isn't the constraint; contention for the GPU is [H]. Use one priority queue with a GPU semaphore, ordered interactive chat, then ASR, then per-conversation extraction, then nightly batch, with batch preemptible between items. Measure the ASR real-time factor alone and with concurrent Ollama load before tuning [M].

**ASR risk:** Whisper tends to mishandle intra-sentence code-switching by translating or dropping the minority language [H]. Compare language auto-detect against forced language, and against a Vietnamese-tuned Whisper variant, on your gold set [M]. A personal glossary in the initial prompt is the cheap win to try before any fine-tuning.

**Challenges to the four components:**

- **Vector database: no.** A year of data is tens of thousands of chunks. SQLite FTS5 plus embeddings in a table (sqlite-vec or brute force) is enough. The real question is whether your embedding model retrieves across Vietnamese and English [M].
- **Graph database: no.** Entity and relation tables in SQLite will do.
- **Full diarization or identification: defer.** But wearer versus not-wearer tagging is needed now. Enrol the wearer's voice and score each segment as wearer, other or unknown. Without it, every memory is potentially misattributed, which violates your own principle.
- **Fine-tuning: defer** indefinitely. Corrections and deletions cannot cascade into model weights.

## 3. Evidence-backed memory

**Three layers with explicit dependency edges:**

- **L0 sources:** audio spans and versioned transcript segments.
- **L1 atomic claims:** each carries the fields below.
  - Type: `observed_event`, `self_report`, `other_report` or `hypothesis`.
  - Speaker: wearer, other or unknown, with confidence.
  - Evidence: segment ID, transcript version, character span and audio time.
  - Lifecycle: `valid_from` and `review_by`, plus a status of proposed, active, superseded or rejected.
  - Provenance: model and prompt version.
- **L2 derived artifacts:** summaries, project and person pages, goals. Each lists its input claim IDs.

**Rules [R]:**

- An unknown speaker can never produce a `self_report`. An `other_report` is never treated as the wearer's belief.
- A hypothesis needs at least three independent pieces of evidence across at least two days. It is never evidence for another hypothesis and never becomes fact without user confirmation.
- **Correction:** a new transcript version marks dependent claims stale, then dependent L2 artifacts stale, with lazy regeneration. This extends your existing invalidation [F].
- **Deletion:** hard-delete the source, claims, FTS rows, embeddings, Mac job results and backups, then regenerate L2. Keep only a hash tombstone so re-sync cannot resurrect it. The Mac retaining results after audio removal [F] is a deletion gap today.
- **Expiration by type:** states such as "stressed about X" expire in days, projects in weeks unless re-observed, and biographical facts persist.
- **Contradictions:** keep both, time-stamped. A newer wearer self-report supersedes an older one for preferences. Otherwise surface it only when it affects an output.
- **Review burden:** per-memory approval will not scale. Auto-accept low-stakes claims as "unreviewed", visibly marked. Require approval for health, relationships, third parties and all hypotheses. Cap the queue at about 10 items a day and track the acceptance rate; if it falls below about 70%, fix extraction rather than asking for more review.

## 4. How it improves

"Improves" here means context and glossary growth, not model learning.

- **Day 1:** a corrected glossary of names and project terms feeds ASR and extraction. The daily review lists commitments you made, decisions and open questions, each with tap-to-play evidence.
- **Week 1:** recurring projects and people get pages. The system tracks which commitments were followed through and where time went by topic. A weekly review is pulled by you, not pushed.
- **Month 1:** it reports trends in what you said, comparisons of stated priorities against observed time, and recurring blockers.

**Feedback template:** observation, count, quotes, alternative explanations and a question. For example: "In 7 of 9 conversations about project X you said some version of 'no time'. That could be workload, or the recorded sample. Is X still a priority?" It never says "you are anxious" or "you are a procrastinator", and affect appears only as quoted self-report.

**Notifications:** none by default. Allow one scheduled digest and user-defined triggers.

**Dependency:** don't measure engagement. Measure commitments closed, correction rate and rated usefulness. Use no affection-simulating language, and give feedback that points outward to people and actions.

## 5. Vertical slice, gates and objections

**Slice:** Opus capture, incremental store, background upload, stitched ASR with wearer tagging, extraction of commitments and entities with evidence, a daily review screen, and correct/delete cascade.

**Gates:**

| Area | Proposed gate |
|---|---|
| Energy | On a scripted 4-hour session, never reach `thermalState` "serious", and drain stays within about 1.5× a BLE-connected idle baseline |
| Bilingual accuracy | On at least 2 hours of your own hand-corrected audio, report WER/CER separately for Vietnamese, English, code-switched and noisy strata, plus named-entity recall and edge-versus-mid-section error; set thresholds after the baseline |
| Offline recovery | Under fault injection (app kill, phone reboot, 8-hour Mac outage, mid-drain disconnect), zero lost and zero duplicated seconds by sequence continuity, with recovery needing no user action |
| Grounded personalization | Every claim resolves to playable evidence; sampled claim precision is at least 90% by your judgment; wearer misattribution is under 5%; after deletion, FTS, embeddings and summaries return zero hits; weekly review takes at most 10 minutes |

**Tradeoffs:**

- Opus-only storage makes a future phone ASR fallback and playback slightly harder.
- The Mac becomes a single point of failure and needs backup.
- Auto-accepted claims trade review burden for error risk.

**Strongest objections:**

1. Always-on capture of third parties is a consent and legal problem that architecture doesn't solve.
2. Errors compound from ASR through attribution and extraction into memory, so "mentor" output may rest on noise.
3. Most daily audio is low-value, and a deliberate voice-note habit might deliver 80% of the benefit.
4. Review fatigue could kill the correction loop that the design depends on.

## Two assumptions to challenge

1. **That ASR was the phone's heat source.** If the O(N) encrypted saves and UI churn dominate, the move to the Mac bought little and the phone will get worse as the library grows.
2. **That transcripts are a good enough foundation for memory.** That means code-switched accuracy plus wearer attribution. If either fails its gate, everything in sections 3 and 4 inherits the error, and the right investment is ASR and attribution, not memory features.

CLAUDE FOLLOW-UP RESPONSE

## Verdicts

I accept all six, with two refinements.

1. **Accept.** Mandatory enrollment was wrong for the MVP, and it does permit a smaller slice. Attribution should come by construction: a user-initiated voice note is the wearer's, and an ambient passage becomes theirs only when confirmed. Unconfirmed ambient speech stays searchable but can never update the profile.
2. **Accept.** My sequence-range key and "delete after receipt" were both unsafe.
   - **Key:** device ID, recording epoch, sequence and content hash.
   - **Switch:** on passing equivalence and recovery tests, not on a calendar.
   - **Phone deletion:** off until archive receipt, retention policy and a tested restore all exist.
3. **Accept.** I withdraw the three-observation threshold, "where time went" and commitment follow-through from mentions. My refinement is to still show counts, deduplicated by distinct source span and labelled as mentions, not as confidence.
4. **Accept.** "Hard-delete backups" was overstated. Your semantics are right, provided restore applies tombstones before any indexing or derivation and the user is told the backup expiry window. My refinement is that per-row encryption leaves indexes, FTS and the WAL in plaintext, so page-level encryption (SQLCipher) fits better. For migration, reuse the existing Keychain key and keep the old blob until the new store verifies.
5. **Accept.** "Memory isn't the constraint" was too strong.
   - **Scheduling:** at job granularity with small ASR units, since in-flight generations can't be assumed preemptible.
   - **Fairness:** a bounded chat burst budget plus aging, so ASR always progresses.
   - **Chat:** source-linked chat moves out of "defer"; the persona stays deferred.
6. **Accept.** I'm taking your availability check as given, since I haven't verified it. Compare it with the 8B on your gold set for bilingual grounded answers and for latency under load.

## Revised sequence

Steps 3–5 run on today's WAV path, so they need not wait for step 2.

1. **Storage:** profiling, then the incremental encrypted store with crash-safe migration.
2. **Capture integrity:** the epoch-and-hash-keyed Opus container, preserving packet timing, gap markers and decoder warm-up, dual-written alongside WAV. The Mac gets a durable archive with receipt and verified backup.
3. **Mac pipeline:** the async topic queue, the scheduler with admission control, and the gold set.
4. **Explicit profile:** a goals and preferences screen plus a voice-note mode, both attributed by construction.
5. **Source-linked chat:** answers cite playable spans; ambient speakers are shown as unattributed.
6. **Confirmation and cascade:** a confirmed passage becomes a claim; correction and deletion cascade with tombstones.
7. **Later:** optional voice enrollment, used only to pre-sort confirmation suggestions and never as definitive.
